When a system must make a bounded decision, it can implement that decision as a rule, a conventional classifier, a typed decision model, or a generative language model. Those primitives differ in quality and in operational behavior. A natural hope is that measurable properties of the task, fixed before any primitive is scored, would predict that behavior on a task the predictor has not seen.

We report an eight-task pilot that freezes the task set, the fingerprint, the primitive implementations, and the predictor before the predictive results are known. The target is a seven-coordinate profile: macro-F1, local training seconds, local inference seconds, API dollars, per-example latency at the 50th and 95th percentiles, and the invalid-output rate. The predictor is seven independent ridge regressions with penalty~1. The baseline is the mean profile of the same primitive on the tasks used to fit that fold. ESCI is held out of every fit as an e-commerce domain. Other holdouts remove a task family. Several of those families contain only one task.

On the measured matrix, quality and validity vary sharply by primitive and task. Under the pinned Qwen2.5-0.5B configuration, the invalid-output rate runs from 0.023 and 0.025 on VitaminC and SMS Spam to 1 on Civil Comments, where macro-F1 is 0 because every output failed the one-label rule. The ridge model is closer than the primitive mean on macro-F1 for 5 of 8 held-out tasks and on the invalid rate for 2 of 8. Under every vector policy, both predictors select the classifier. On ESCI, macro-F1 error falls from 0.2825 to 0.1024 and invalid-rate error from 0.1199 to 0.0444, and the selected primitive does not change.

Eight tasks do not establish that task fingerprints are unpredictive in general, that one primitive is best, or that latency on this rig is a hardware-normalized speed. The rule, the classifier, and the decision model were timed on CPU. The generative model was timed on a GTX 1650 Ti in float16. API dollars are 0 because no API was called.
